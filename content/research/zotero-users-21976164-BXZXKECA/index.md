It traces the influence of Ruskin, Pugin and Morris; the role of exhibitions, guilds, schools and retailers; and the movement’s contributions to interiors, architecture and rural craft production\. ## Key findings

- The source characterises Arts and Crafts as an ideals-led movement seeking well-made everyday objects and less dehumanising production, rather than a prescriptive style\.
- It dates the founding of the Arts and Crafts Exhibition Society to 1887 and its first exhibition to 1888, identifying exhibitions as important platforms for decorative artists\.
- It distinguishes Morris’s criticism of the division of labour from outright opposition to machinery\.
- It describes schools, guilds, manufacturers and retailers as mechanisms for spreading craft skills and reaching wider audiences\.
- It connects architects’ involvement with integrated interior design and work across multiple craft disciplines\.
- It describes an urban-origin movement that developed rural workshops, revived local skills and persisted longer in the countryside\.
- It portrays declining British momentum after the First World War alongside influence on European Modernism and continued institutional activity\.
