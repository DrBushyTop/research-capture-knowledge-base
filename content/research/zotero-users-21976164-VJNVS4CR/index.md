# Focal Plant Observations as a Standardised Method for Pollinator Monitoring: Opportunities and Limitations for Mass Participation Citizen Science

## Source facts

Helen E\. Roy, Elizabeth Baxter, Aoine Saunders, Michael J\. O\. Pocock

DOI: 10\.1371/journal\.pone\.0150794

<https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0150794>

## Machine interpretation

This 2016 research article evaluates the Big Bumblebee Discovery, a UK citizen-science project using timed observations of lavender during June–August 2014\. An estimated 13,024 primary-school children contributed 3,948 reports containing 26,868 reported bumblebees\. Reported visitation rates were lower when lavender was more than 5 m from other flowers and higher in sunny conditions; wider landscape category was not significantly associated with reported abundance\. Substantial identification errors prevented analysis of bumblebee colour-group richness\. The study demonstrates the reach of a simple monitoring protocol but highlights the need to assess data accuracy rather than assume it\.

## Key findings

- The project received 3,948 reports from 1,561 unique 100 m grid-square locations; 3,556 reports were valid for analysis\.
- Lavender more than 5 m from other flowers had an approximately halved reported bumblebee visitation rate relative to lavender next to other flowers \(P \< 0\.001\)\.
- Reported visitation was approximately twice as high in full sun as in no sun \(P \< 0\.001\)\.
- Wider landscape category \(P = 0\.157\), wind \(P = 0\.131\), and lavender species \(P = 0\.945\) were not significantly associated with reported visitation\.
- In the submitted photographic subset, approximately 75% of insects reported as bumblebees were actually bumblebees\. Colour-group identification precision was approximately 43–44%, compared with 57% in a separate photo quiz\.
- Identification uncertainty led the authors to analyse total reported individuals rather than colour-group richness\.

## Limitations

- The observational design supports associations, not causal conclusions about floral proximity or weather\.
- Only 83 photographs were uploaded\. This small, self-selected verification subset may not represent identification accuracy across all reports\.
- Analysis of reported abundance assumed that bumblebee misidentification rates were randomly distributed across observations; this assumption was not established\.
- Colour groups are proxies rather than species-level identifications, and errors prevented testing the intended richness hypothesis\.
- Prior participant knowledge and use of educational resources were not measured\. No additional or face-to-face training was provided\.
- The separate identification quiz involved only 27 adults and children and did not reproduce all field-observation conditions\.
- Participation was estimated rather than directly counted: the authors give a potential range of 4,646–30,077 participants\.
- The source contains inconsistent school counts: the abstract says over 4,000 schools, whereas the results document 400 unique school codes and estimate participation from at least 400 schools\.
- Sampling density varied geographically, with fewer reports in areas of low human population density\. A single field season cannot establish long-term pollinator trends\.
- There were too few combinations of explanatory factors to robustly model their interactions\. Lavender species was added as a post hoc hypothesis\.
- Non-significant results do not establish that landscape, wind, or lavender species have no ecological effect\.

## Why this is relevant

- Useful for designing mass-participation pollinator surveys and choosing identification tasks appropriate to volunteer experience\.
- Provides a cautionary example of how taxonomic errors can invalidate diversity outcomes even when a monitoring protocol is simple\.
- Relevant to research on local floral context and flower visitation, while distinguishing visitation counts from population abundance\.
- Illustrates the value and limitations of photographic verification and independent identification assessments\.
- Relevant to school-based citizen science, hypothesis-led engagement, and evaluation of participant recruitment\.
